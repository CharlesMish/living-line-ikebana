# Fourth guest pair — direction

Working note for the Grok arrangements, written after reading the three guest
pairs and after placing, aiming, and bending cuttings in the app. The bowls
are judged against the stop line at the bottom. This note does not change the
behavioral contract.

## What the three pairs already are

Each pair is a scene held in or over the vessel.

- Fable, *After the Leaves*: a pruned bare branch, silver seed, and berry twig
  in an offset charcoal bowl. What remains after cutting. *The Water Between*:
  two parasol leaves and a reed over open water. The new cutting is a round
  blade held away from its root.
- Argon, *Moon-Rise Over Dark Water*: a mixed evening picture (bare branch,
  silver seed, lobed leaf, nodding flower, starry pincushion) on a bench
  against mist hills. *Lanterns Across the Ford*: two bowls and a new hanging
  lantern spray bridging the gap.
- Astra, *The Last Ferry*: reeds, one broad leaf, and silver seed crossing
  between two bowls. *Under the Copper Beech*: lobed leaves, nodding flowers,
  and an arching trailer as a warm mass in a small iron bowl.

Reed intervals already have their own study bowl (“Reed lines and the spaces
between”). A fourth stand of reeds would repeat that study and Astra’s lines.

## What the tools did when I tried them

Unshaped, in the original bowl, from Front and three-quarter:

- The single flower is one cream face on a long stem. None of the six bowls
  uses it. Aiming the stem from Front lays that face out past the side rim
  and keeps the stock length. Pressing the bloom turns the face without
  moving the stem. The face cannot be shortened: it is the tip, and prune
  takes the tip.
- The broad leaf will lie flat, and then it is a blade that crosses the bowl.
  Fable already recorded that and made the parasol instead. I am not making a
  second round leaf.
- The leafy shoot’s bend is a modest arc. A press on the middle takes the
  bend bead; the leaves make the stem hard to aim.
- The fern takes a lean. The arching trailer already lives near the water; a
  further bend puts its tip through the water.
- The flowering branch, foliage fan, and indigo spire read clearly, and the
  last of those is already the left bowl of *Lanterns Across the Ford*.

The move none of the three made: a line that leaves the ceramic and offers
its face to the person on the near side.

## The pair

Two ways of arranging the same face.

1. **The flower leaves.** One single flower, aimed so the stem crosses the
   water and the bloom passes the near rim, toward the person who would be
   sitting across the table. The first view is that person’s view. Anything
   else in the bowl has to be quieter than the face. Working title: *Past the Rim*.
2. **The flower stays.** The same cutting, kept inside the rim. The line that
   moves is a different one — a fern or a leafy shoot, bent, still in the
   bowl — so the face is held among leaves instead of given away. Working
   title: *Still in the Bowl*.

The guide’s unused brief (“a lower arrangement for a table where people will
talk across it”) belongs to the first bowl. The second bowl is the other
decision: arrange for the bowl, not for the conversation.

No parasol, lantern, silver seed, lobed maple, two-bowl gap, or offset
charcoal evening. Those are their pictures. Vessel, wall, and light should
stay plain enough that the face is the event.

## A new cutting

Not unless the second bowl, after the fern and the leafy shoot have both been
tried, still asks for a form those two cannot make. A low fan of narrow blades
was the form I noticed the palette does not have. It is not the pair. The
pair is one face leaving and one face staying. If the stock lines can say
that, a new generator would be a third idea.

## Sufficient to stop

- From the kept view, *Past the Rim* shows the bloom beyond the near rim and
  the stem crossing water. The face is large enough to read on a garden card.
  The bowl is not a bouquet.
- *Still in the Bowl* keeps that same face inside the rim. The bent line does
  not repeat the first bowl’s diagonal. If the leafy shoot cannot be aimed
  cleanly, the fern is the staying line, and the note says so.
- Both backups use only generators already on current main, so either one
  imports into the current app.
- One sentence per bowl about a change made after seeing it rendered.
- One obstacle, the one that most affected the work.
- Stop there. Do not add a cutting to make the pair more impressive.

## What was kept

Both bowls are a compact chalk glaze bowl, photographed on a sage wall, low
stone, window light, landscape, at 1.18×. The same single-flower cutting
(seed 8278) is the face in each. No new generator.

*Past the Rim* (`past-the-rim.garden.json`, `past-the-rim.png`) is one single
flower. The base sits on the far side of the water. Aimed from the side, the
stem crosses the bowl and the bloom passes the near rim. The first view is
that near side. From above, the bloom is outside the ceramic.

*Still in the Bowl* (`still-in-the-bowl.garden.json`,
`still-in-the-bowl.png`) is the same flower left standing, plus one fern
frond. The flower’s face stays inside the rim. The fern is pruned and bent
so it remains beside the stem, over the water, shorter than the flower.

The leafy shoot was the first candidate for that staying line. A press on
its middle took the bend bead, and the leaves made the stem hard to aim.
The fern took the lean, so the fern is the staying line.

Revised after seeing them rendered:

- *Past the Rim*: the chosen view first showed the back of the bloom. Turning
  the face up, until the status read “Turn the flower face,” is the change.
  Looking from above, and from the other side, did not move the plant.
- *Still in the Bowl*: the first fern bend crossed the flower’s stem. The
  fern was pruned and the bend reduced so the two lines stay apart, and the
  face was turned after it read edge-on.

The obstacle that most affected the work: aim moves the grabbed point in the
camera-facing plane. The flower could be sent toward the sitter only by
aiming from the side and then coming back to look. A second limit, not the
one that decided the bowls: pruning a single flower takes the bloom, so the
face cannot be shortened.

Both backups contain only `single-flower-v1` and `fern-frond-v2`. Either one
imports into the current app on its own. The branch adds the two Garden
cards beside the six; it does not change a generator.

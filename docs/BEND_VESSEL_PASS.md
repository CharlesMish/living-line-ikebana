Charlie requested slightly more bend and reported mistaking the water/vessel for
an effect that was switched off. This is direct player evidence for a readability

## Implemented

The shared bend solver permits 20% more input travel and terminal rotation before
saturating. Small-drag gain, the 54% bead, broad influence span, stiffness ordering,
rest lengths, attachments and snapshot-based editing are unchanged. Existing saved
shapes load exactly as stored; the extra range applies to subsequent gestures.
This adds travel at the existing bend point, not a second independently movable
curvature station. It is an artistic control limit, not a claim about real stems'
fracture strength or springback.

The former capped cylinder is replaced by a hollow lathed bowl with an inner wall,
basin floor and raised lip. The water meets the inner wall below the lip and has a
stronger teal value against warm ceramic. A lighter lip and restrained glaze make
the edge distinct. The kenzan body reaches the new basin floor while its top stays at the same height.
Pin field, insertion coordinates and cameras stay unchanged.
No decorative waves, water meter or correctness overlay are introduced.

Focused numerical tests check small-drag response, extended saturation, both
materials' retained lengths and attachments, and ray intersections with the basin
floor, inward wall and lip. These do not constitute a rendered or physical-phone
sign-off. The preview browser previously rejected the local build; no new visual
verification is claimed here.

## Proposed game loop — discussion only

A compact commission loop could work: receive a request and a small material kit,
arrange and revise, choose a presentation view, deliver, and receive a new option.
Keep making for yourself available alongside commissions. Begin with three short
requests using the current two materials before building an economy.

Early tutorials can show an example and teach one action (turn a continuation,
remove a leaf, reveal an open shape). Let example images be references rather than
pixel-matching tests: varied three-dimensional arrangements can meet the same
request. Later requests should specify a setting or intention, such as a low
arrangement for a dinner conversation, a visible sweep of water, or two lines that
respond to each other. If requirements are enforced, make them few, clear and
observable; do not hide a beauty score behind client satisfaction.

For the first trial, finishing a request could unlock one option, without money,
quality multipliers or resale optimization. Test whether receiving and interpreting
a request is enjoyable before deciding on prices or a selling loop. A useful new
unlock should offer a new choice; vessels may require different attachment rules,
so do not treat every vessel as a cosmetic swap.

This direction revises the earlier pause on discussing progression in response to
Charlie's explicit question. No missions, completion rules, sales, rewards or new
materials are implemented in this pass.

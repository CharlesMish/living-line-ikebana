# Living Line — short brief for Grok and Claude

**Historical review request.** The combined responses led to
[Looking refinement](LOOKING_REFINEMENT.md). In particular, the bare-twig-first
and fixed-kit proposals below are deferred; neither is the current extension plan.

Please critique the direction below. Aim for 400–600 words, answer the questions,
and offer one concrete disagreement or alternative. Distinguish verified ikebana
practice from game-design judgment; cite sources for school-specific claims.

## Current prototype

A shallow bowl, water and kenzan; persistent cuttings that you place, aim, bend and
prune. Arrange edits material; Step Back offers Orbit/Move and zoom. Cuts remove
actual distal material. Edits preserve identity and stock length; cancellation
rolls back. Two initial references now coexist: a woody flowering branch and a
more yielding leafy shoot. Camera feel and the pair's visual tuning still need
physical-phone review. There is no score, curriculum or complete game loop yet.

## Astra's proposed direction

Make a quiet creative game about **noticing a line, choosing what to reveal, and
committing a composition**. Borrow the accessible hand-to-material relationship
from pottery games; let the plant's existing structure shape the decisions.
Unlike a continuously moldable lump, this material has attachment points, leaf
faces, finite branch lengths and consequential cuts.

Start with moribana's shallow-vessel workbench and take inspiration from Sogetsu's
openness to personal expression. These are influences, not a claim to teach either
school faithfully. School forms could support optional, clearly attributed studies
later. They need not become compulsory graph roles or a universal correctness score.
Water, visible insertion space and the gaps between stems should matter to looking
and arranging, including from Above.

Keep the palette narrow in species count but diverse in decisions: woody branching,
leaf rhythm, bare line, then a distinct flower face. Each material should change
what the player notices or chooses to remove. Let the two references establish
shared behavior before commissioning one bounded bare-twig candidate.

For a first game-loop experiment, consider one optional study with a small fixed
set of cuttings and an open prompt such as “reveal one leaning line.” Let players
finish when satisfied, keep a view of their arrangement, and revisit the same
materials. Compare that with fully open play before choosing progression. This
study, a finish/collection feature, and any finite supply are proposals, not built
features. Do not add them as part of a material prototype.

## Questions

1. What would make you want a second ten-minute session? Propose one small loop
   that adds a meaningful decision, and name what you would remove to make room.
2. Where does the pottery-game analogy help, and where would it teach the wrong
   expectation about living material? Give one concrete interaction example.
3. Is this combination of a moribana workbench and expressive freedom coherent?
   Which one optional study would ground it in ikebana, and what should remain
   open? Identify any school distinctions we are flattening.
4. After the flowering branch and leafy shoot, which single material would produce
   the most new choices for the least new machinery? Challenge the bare-twig-first
   proposal if another example is stronger.
5. What observation during a phone playtest would most strongly falsify this
   direction? Include whether Move reads as reframing the scene or editing a plant,
   and whether the water/negative space actually informs a decision.

## Reference anchors

explains the shallow container and kenzan and also notes school-specific forms.

before presenting any formal lesson as instruction.

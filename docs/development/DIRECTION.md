# Direction after this handoff

## Built now

Two reference materials, shared persistent editing, optional looking prompts,
Stop and look, and Garden's keep/revisit/copy/fresh-bowl loop. No score, missions,
formal curriculum, finite supply, economy or unlock sequence is implemented.

## Preferred next learning structure — proposal

Distinguish a **lesson** (one idea worth understanding) from an **exercise**
(a short opportunity to try it). A lesson can have several exercises and more than
one material variation. Begin with our own small exercises that teach the tool and
attention together; do not claim these reproduce a school's lesson sequence.

| Lesson idea | First exercise | Later variation | Question at stopping |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Read a line | One cutting; find its direction and face | Same cutting from another view | What is already doing useful work? |
| Reveal space | Preview one cut, then keep or cancel it | Compare another fork/leaf | What would the cut reveal? |
| A second voice | Two cuttings, one clear relationship | Swap one material role | Where does the eye move next? |
| Water and footprint | Look from Above, slide one base | Return to the intended front | What shape of water remains? |
| Keep and return | Save a moment; make a copy if curious | Compare another solution later | Does another change add anything? |

These are game-design proposals, not sourced statements about ikebana doctrine.
They should permit stopping without a required cut or decorative density. The
collection offers a reason to return without inventing a correctness score.

Later, a source-grounded introductory **form** could become a longer lesson with
these exercises as preparation. Do not equate a fully developed teaching photograph
with a mandatory beginner stem count. Research what the teacher actually repeats,
varies and requires before specifying a game version. Keep school-specific terms
and proportions distinct; record uncertainty where public sources are incomplete.

The research deliverable should name an exact source, its teaching objective,
verified constraints, example variation, missing information, attribution/permission
questions, and our original exercise proposal. Facts and ideas may inform design;
public availability is not permission to reproduce lesson text or images. Obtain
appropriate permission before any close adaptation or school/teacher affiliation
claim. No need to settle this to evaluate two material prototypes.

A first pass of that research lives in
[LESSON_SOURCE_LEDGER.md](LESSON_SOURCE_LEDGER.md): one introductory form
(moribana upright / *chokuritsu-kei*), cited public pages, required-vs-variant
notes, permission gaps, and original exercise proposals. It is documentation
only; it does not add an in-app lesson.

## Palette and progression

Six to eight material roles can be a useful first collection, not a permanent cap.
Evaluate new roles by differences in line, branching, leaf rhythm, flower face,
mass and useful cutting decisions. More color variations alone do not establish
more play. Test several copies together before treating variety as a species count.

A future seasonal prompt or request could invite a return to the Garden. Prototype
one original invitation first, without currency or unlocks: make, keep, and optionally
revisit a previous arrangement. Only expand the loop if that invitation gives a
reason to compose again. Broad-leaf deformation and another vessel remain separate
mechanical projects; materials should not smuggle them into this round.
